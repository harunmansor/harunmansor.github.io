I have always liked the idea of being comfortable in the water, but spearfishing made that feeling different. It was not just about swimming, holding my breath, or going down. It was about slowing everything down enough to notice what was happening around me.

This was my first real experience trying spearfishing, and I quickly learned that it is not as simple as just diving and aiming at a fish.

## Getting into the water

At first, I was mostly trying to settle myself. The ocean has a way of reminding you that you are only a guest. Every movement felt louder than it should be, and every breath before a dive felt important.

I had to think about the basics:

- staying relaxed
- moving slowly
- equalizing properly
- watching my surroundings
- not rushing the dive

The hardest part was not the physical effort. It was staying calm enough to make good decisions.

## What surprised me

The biggest surprise was how much patience spearfishing needs. Fish do not appear just because you are ready. You have to observe, wait, and learn how the environment behaves.

I also started to understand how freediving skills connect with spearfishing. A good duck dive, relaxed finning, and clean equalization make everything smoother. When one of those things is off, the whole dive feels messy.

## What I learned

My first spearfishing experience taught me that comfort underwater matters more than confidence on land. It is easy to think you are ready before entering the water, but the ocean gives honest feedback.

Some lessons I want to remember:

- do not force a dive
- do not chase too aggressively
- stay aware of depth, current, and buddy position
- respect the animal and the environment
- improve the fundamentals before thinking about results

## Closing thoughts

I came out of the water with more respect for spearfishing than before. It combines freediving, hunting, patience, awareness, and responsibility in one activity.

For now, I am still at the beginning. But it feels like something worth learning properly.
